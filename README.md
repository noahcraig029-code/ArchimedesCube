## The Archimedes Cube 
I added magnet indents to each of the cube pieces which allowed for a more uniform shape that enabled the marble to travel more smoothly between mazes, as well as organized each individual part to create simple printing processes. I also added a doc to guide users through the printing and assembly. 

## Part List

* 1 Core — The central structural foundation.
* 6 Swivels — The center mechanism pieces.
* 8 Corners — Outer corner puzzle pieces.
* 12 Sides — Outer edge puzzle pieces.
* Maze Panels — External tracks for the marble.
* Edge/Corner Framing Pieces — Outer tunnel walls for marble transit.

## Assembly Instructions

Sand down each individual piece before starting the assembly. Removing printing artifacts reduces friction and ensures the cube turns smoothly.
Insert the 6 swivels into the central core. Slide the side pieces into the grooves created by the swivel bases. If you are using magnets, double-check that their poles are correctly oriented to attract the magnet pieces before securing them. Insert the corner pieces in between the side pieces. Assemble the cube layer by layer, starting with the bottom and moving to the middle. Build the final layer up, following the structure of a traditional Rubik's cube. Do not put a corner or side piece in last, as forcing them into place can damage the internal structure. Always insert a center swivel piece last to ensure a tight, efficient turn and clasp.
Before gluing anything further, turn the un-mazed cube around for a bit to break in the plastic and verify that the mechanism rotates properly.

Once the base cube turns well, you can mount the external maze tracks. Use liquid superglue to put the maze faces onto the center swivels. Make sure you don’t accidentally glue the pieces of the cube together! Check out the Onshape link to find out more!
